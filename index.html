Earlier study: did “scoring below usage” work?

Reproduced October 16, 2026. We rebuilt the Week 5 screen in 2021–2025: usage points at least 2 above actual PPR per game, with at least 3 recorded appearances. Only Weeks 1–5 determined the flag. We then compared later regular-season scoring.

Among 1,556 eligible player-seasons, 220 were flagged. They averaged 9.1 PPR per game before the cutoff and 10.7 afterward, against 12.4 usage points. About two-thirds improved, but this is not a prediction-accuracy percentage.

For context, we fitted a line relating early scoring to later scoring among unflagged players at the same position in the same season. Flagged players finished 1.8 PPR per game above that fitted comparison. This comparison uses later outcomes to fit the line: it is retrospective evidence, not a forecast trained only on earlier seasons.

The average difference from the fitted comparison was positive in all five seasons: +1.0, +2.3, +1.2, +1.6 and +2.8 PPR per game. Repeated resampling of flagged observations gives a pooled range of about +1.3 to +2.4. This simplified range does not account for repeated players, shared seasons or uncertainty in the comparison line; it is not a guaranteed future advantage.

What we learned: the screen found a group that improved, but did not justify projecting every player at his full usage estimate. Simply using current PPG was closer to later scoring for this flagged group: an average miss of 3.4 points per game versus 3.7 using usage. No weights were changed to make this result look better.

Important limit: eligibility requires at least four later recorded appearances. Players who missed most of the remaining season are left out, so these results do not establish a strategy's injury-adjusted payoff. The dynasty age filter was not applied; player-seasons can repeat the same person. This is one fixed Week 5 screen, not a top-15 portfolio or a new untouched test.

Which improvements are actually running?

Data caveat: the original study fills unmatched opportunity rows with zero. Most unmatched rows also have zero actual points, but 22 nonzero-scoring player-weeks across the five full seasons have no opportunity match. Missing opportunity is not necessarily zero opportunity. This needs a coverage-sensitivity check before treating the result as evidence for a stronger prediction model.

Opportunity is not a promise

A player may have more rushing, receiving or passing opportunity than his scoring suggests. But targets near the goal line, deep misses, unusual game situations and a small sample can explain the difference. The detail panel shows those facts rather than calling every positive gap a bargain.

Compare the right time horizons

Redraft asks who can help this season. Dynasty also asks about future years. A productive veteran can be a useful short-term rental without becoming a developmental breakout. Being young is not enough either: talent and a sustained role still matter.

Keep the test honest

At each historical cutoff, inputs are limited to what was known through that week, then compared with later scoring. Some tests train on earlier seasons and evaluate a later season. Multiple cutoffs can repeat players and overlap future games; they should not be counted as independent discoveries.

Active-game averages exclude missed appearances. Calendar-week evaluations can count absences and answer a different question. Previously inspected seasons are retrospective evidence, not a new untouched test.

About Fantasy Terminal

Player workload, scoring and context for fantasy decisions.

What to use it for

Compare a player's scoring with the opportunities he receives, check whether his role is changing, and review injuries, market references and upcoming opponents. Select a player for his recent usage, scoring history and the reasons behind the numbers.

Keep the time horizon clear

Redraft focuses on this season. Dynasty adds age, draft background and separately dated market references. A productive veteran can help a lineup now without having the same long-term value as a younger player. The scoring model is not a career forecast.

Read the evidence before acting

A usage gap is a reason to investigate, not an automatic buy or sell. More opportunities can help, but QB play, efficiency, health and competition can keep scoring below the usage estimate. The Research page shows what historical tests found, including changes that failed to improve predictions.

Method & development

Built with Python and nflreadpy, using AI-assisted development. Sources, calculations and test limitations are documented. New metrics are evaluated before they can change production estimates.

Sources

Professional-player statistics are derived from saved nflverse data through nflreadpy. Usage-based points use ffopportunity: receiving for WR/TE, rushing plus receiving for RB, and passing plus rushing for QB. Snap counts originate with PFR via nflverse. Source samples can differ, and missing fields remain unavailable.
